Global KYC Is Not a Translation Exercise

Global consistency does not mean copying one jurisdiction’s rules everywhere. It means creating a common system capable of understanding local context.

Micheal SheehyExecutive perspectives on payments, compliance and adaptive governance.← All insights

The hardest part of global KYC is not translating policy language. It is preserving local regulatory meaning inside a shared operating model.

One standard can still create inconsistency

A global policy may look consistent on paper while producing different outcomes across markets. Definitions, documentary expectations, ownership rules, data availability and regulator practice vary.

Imposing the strictest jurisdiction everywhere can feel safe, but it often creates unnecessary friction without improving actual risk understanding.

Architecture first, localization second

A scalable model begins with common architecture: shared customer objects, common risk concepts, controlled workflows, consistent evidence and transparent decision rights.

Localization should then occur through governed rules, data requirements and escalation paths—not through separate systems and undocumented manual workarounds.

Local expertise is part of the control

Translation can tell you what a rule says. Local expertise helps determine how it is interpreted, what evidence regulators expect and where judgment is permitted.

The objective is not uniformity for its own sake. It is harmonization: one operating system, common governance and enough local intelligence to make the system accurate.
