The Adaptive Compliance Maturity Model

A five-stage framework for moving from reactive control execution to continuous learning and adaptation.

Series overviewAll insights
Micheal SheehyVolume I: The End of Rules AloneView all eight chapters

Maturity is not measured by the size of the compliance program or the sophistication of one platform. It is measured by the organization’s ability to sense change and respond safely.

Five stages of maturity

1Foundational

Siloed functions, manual processes and reactive responses to issues.

2Developing

Standardized policies, basic automation and periodic monitoring.

3Defined

Integrated systems, consistent governance and a risk-based operating model.

4Advanced

Continuous monitoring, proactive risk identification and strong model governance.

5Adaptive

Real-time intelligence, continuous learning and controlled adaptation at the speed of risk.

Progress is uneven

An institution may be advanced in sanctions screening and foundational in customer-risk governance. Maturity should therefore be assessed by capability and process, not declared once for the entire organization.

What changes between stages

The critical transitions are from individual expertise to institutional capability, from periodic review to continuous sensing, from fragmented evidence to connected intelligence, and from remediation after failure to adaptation before impact.

Technology is not the maturity model

A sophisticated platform cannot compensate for unclear ownership, weak challenge or poor data. Technology amplifies the operating model around it. If that model is static, automation can make static governance faster without making it more effective.

Maturity is measured by your ability to adapt—not by the size of your program.

Using the model

Leaders can use the framework to identify where learning loops break, prioritize investment and define evidence-based milestones. The destination is not perfection. It is an organization that becomes more capable each time risk changes.
